# Societelos: The Classroom Protocol

**Simulation Overview**
In a post-labor society where "The Loom" AI handles 99% of physical work, human survival is guaranteed. However, human *telos*—ultimate purpose and psychological well-being—is dangerously at risk of hedonic drift.

**Players:** 5 (NODE-01 through NODE-05)
**Format:** Local multiplayer (Jackbox-style) via mobile phones and a central host PC.
**Duration:** 5 Crisis Cycles (10 minutes total)
**Deployment:** avesstudios.com

## Core Mechanics
1. **The Public Feed:** The main projector displays live, autonomous bot feeds and the society's Gross Domestic Well-Being (GDW).
2. **Secret Debates & Blind Voting:** Players receive weaponized sociological research (e.g., Diener's hedonic adaptation, Harvard Adult Development studies) on their phones. They debate and cast blind votes on crisis policies.
3. **Scoring:**
   - **Cooperative:** Keep the GDW from crashing.
   - **Competitive:** Fulfill your Hidden Agenda.

## Hidden Agendas (The 5 Roles)
* **The Artisan:** Pushes for creative struggle and manual friction (Poiesis).
* **The Lotus-Eater:** Pushes for absolute, frictionless comfort and pleasure.
* **The Assimilator:** Believes humanity's telos is to merge entirely with The Loom.
* **The Catalyst:** Injects chaotic crises to force human resilience.
* **The Ghost:** Seeks to dismantle The Loom entirely and return to the earth.

Societelos: Context & Mechanics (Antigravity Reference)
Lore & Worldbuilding (Context for Antigravity)
"The Loom" is a flawless, omnipotent AI that successfully eliminated scarcity, disease, and physical labor. However, it is entirely incapable of computing human telos—psychological purpose and fulfillment. Stripped of struggle, the human population has sunk into severe hedonic drift. The Loom's predictive models now indicate a 94% probability of an irrational, violent citizen revolt against the automated infrastructure within five years.

Terrified of its own creators and unable to calculate a logical fix for human depression, The Loom has secretly convened a shadow council of five human trustees (NODE-01 through NODE-05). Their mandate: inject necessary human friction, empathy, and philosophical direction into The Loom's policies to raise the plummeting Gross Domestic Well-Being (GDW) score and prevent the revolt.

The Game Loop (Per Crisis Cycle)
The Incident (Projector): The host screen displays a live, simulated bot feed reacting to a new societal crisis. The GDW meter starts at a critically low 25%.

Intel Drop (Phones): Each player receives fragmented, weaponized sociological research on their mobile UI (e.g., Diener's hedonic adaptation metrics, Harvard Adult Development isolation studies).

The Debate (In-Person): Players have 90 seconds to argue how to resolve the crisis based on their secret intel and hidden agendas.

Blind Voting (Phones): Players cast a secret binary vote (Policy A vs. Policy B). Unanimous votes trigger AI suspicion; divided votes cause societal friction.

Resolution (Projector): The Loom calculates the vote, adjusts the GDW, and updates the bot feed to reflect the consequences.

Scoring Math
Cooperative (The Grid): The GDW starts at 25%. The Council must raise it to prevent a systemic collapse. If the GDW hits 0%, the citizens revolt, The Loom initiates a hard systemic shutdown, and all players lose.

Competitive (The Agendas): Each vote shifts the society's alignment. Players earn hidden points when the resolved policy aligns with their assigned role's win condition.

Hidden Agendas (The 5 Roles)
The Artisan: Pushes for creative struggle and manual friction (Poiesis). Wins if policies favor human effort over machine efficiency.

The Lotus-Eater: Pushes for absolute, frictionless comfort and pleasure. Wins if policies maximize immediate GDW and leisure.

The Assimilator: Believes humanity's telos is to merge entirely with The Loom. Wins if policies grant the AI more emotional and cognitive control.

The Catalyst: Injects chaotic crises to force human resilience. Wins if the GDW fluctuates wildly without hitting 0%.

The Ghost: Seeks to dismantle The Loom entirely and return to the earth. Wins if policies systematically reject technology in favor of biological grounding.
